# Delta: browser-interface

> The change expressed against the current spec as explicit operations.

## MODIFIED

### AC-86 — a second column, on the same terms

**Was:** one column, for the concerns an assessment raised, drawn as a magnifying glass carrying the
count as a badge, with three states kept apart and neither mark conveyed by colour alone.

**Now:** the same, and beside it a second column for what counts FOR the property, drawn as the same
control with a tick in place of the magnifying glass. Everything AC-86 already requires applies to
it without being restated: the three states, the ordinary declared column that sorts, filters and
hides like every other, the origin of its own in the chooser, staying out of the default spreadsheet,
being in the view the table opens on, and every mark being a shape or a texture as well as a colour.

**The same control and not a different one.** The two counts are read together in one movement, and
two differently shaped buttons would read as two unrelated facilities rather than as two halves of
one reading.

**A tick and not a star.** The first column of this table already uses a star and it means *the
person kept this house*. Reusing it for a model's opinion would collapse the two most important
things on the row into one symbol, against the boundary this feature keeps everywhere else.

**What counts for it comes first.** It is the order the questions get asked in, and a column of
worries read first colours the row behind it.

**A property read before the question existed draws an empty cell**, the same as one nothing has
assessed. Those are two different facts in the store, because only one of them is worth spending
money on again, and one fact to a reader: nobody has told them what is good about this house. The
column does not have to carry a distinction that exists for the benefit of a pass. The dialog says
which it is, because there a reader is owed the difference.

The declared-column count becomes forty-five; the default spreadsheet stays at thirty-two.

### AC-87 — the dialog carries both halves

**Was:** pressing the count opens the whole assessment in a dialog: the account, every concern with
its evidence, what each picture showed, what to check before visiting, and what could not be
determined.

**Now:** the same, and what counts for the property with it, headed as its own half so a reader
running down the panel knows which of the two claims they are in. Pressing either control opens the
same dialog, because there is one reading of the house and these are two sections of it.

A reading made before the question existed says so in the dialog, in words, rather than showing a
silence a reader would take for "nothing good about it".

Everything else in AC-87 is unchanged and applies to the second half as written: it is labelled as
the model's and dated, nothing in it is written into the person's own columns, a stale assessment
says so before its content, and the text is fetched when the dialog opens rather than sent with the
table.
